WELCOME

You don't need to figure everything out right now.

Overthinking rarely feels like a problem while you're doing it. It feels like protection. If you just think it through one more time — every way the conversation could have gone, every way this could go wrong — maybe you'll be ready. Maybe nothing will catch you off guard.

But the loop doesn't actually keep you safe. It keeps you tired, second-guessing, and stuck inside a conversation that's already over, or one that hasn't happened yet.

You are not going to spend the next 7 days trying to control every thought. That isn't the goal — and honestly, it doesn't work.

You're going to learn how to notice the spiral, understand what's underneath it, and choose what to do next.

There are no wrong answers. Type it exactly the way your brain says it.

HOW IT WORKS

How this reset works

Seven days. One small idea per day. Each day has an exercise page and a short reflection, so you're never doing more than you can hold.

“This is not about becoming a person who never overthinks.” It is about becoming someone who knows what to do when the spiral starts.

Using this workbook. Tap any box to type. Everything you write saves automatically on this device as you go — there's no file to save and no app to download. Just keep this page bookmarked and come back to it each day.

This workbook is a self-reflection tool. It isn't therapy or medical advice, and it can't replace support from a professional if you need one.

DAY 2

Separate Facts From Fear

One of the sneakiest things overthinking does is make a possibility feel like a fact. The brain fills silence with a story — usually the scariest one — and then treats it like it already happened.

Today you're going to put what you actually know on one side, and what you're afraid of on the other.

DAY 3

Stop Reassurance Hunting

Reassurance hunting is when you go looking outside yourself for certainty — again and again — to quiet a worry. It works for about ten minutes. Then the doubt comes back and you need another hit.

It can look like:

•  Asking friends what they think, repeatedly

•  Checking someone's activity

•  Searching Google for certainty

•  Rereading messages

•  Looking for signs

•  Asking “Are you sure?” over and over

•  Checking whether someone viewed something

Today you're not forcing yourself to stop. You're just noticing when it happens.
